Hello, world! This is the first post on the MaterialX Playground blog.

The Playground started as a reference for the MaterialX node library and has grown into a set of tools that run in your browser: a [Material Viewer]({{ appRoot }}#!viewer), a [Graph Editor]({{ appRoot }}#!graph), [Material Compare]({{ appRoot }}#!compare), a [Scene Viewer]({{ appRoot }}#!scene), an [Embed Builder]({{ appRoot }}#!builder), a [VS Code extension]({{ appRoot }}#!vscode) and a [desktop app]({{ appRoot }}#!desktop). Release notes say what changed. This blog is where we get to say why, and how to make the most of it.

## What we plan to write about

- **Updates.** A plain-language look at new releases: what is new, what got fixed and what it means for your materials.
- **Deep dives.** How parts of the Playground work, from turning a MaterialX document into a shader in your browser to how the viewers render it.
- **Tips and workflows.** Small things that save time in the viewers, the graph editor and the integrations.
- **MaterialX itself.** Notes on the standard, its nodes and its shading models, for people who are new to it and people who ship it.

## Follow along

Subscribe with the [Atom feed]({{ '/feed.xml' | url }}), or check the [roadmap]({{ appRoot }}#!roadmap) to see what is planned. If there is something you would like us to write about, [open an issue on GitHub](https://github.com/joaovbs96/MaterialXPlayground/issues).
