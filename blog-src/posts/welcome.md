The MaterialX Playground has grown from a node library reference into a set of tools: a [Material Viewer]({{ appRoot }}#!viewer), a [Node Graph Editor]({{ appRoot }}#!graph), [Material Compare]({{ appRoot }}#!compare), a Scene Viewer, a gallery, an extension for VS Code and a desktop app. Release notes cover what changed, but not why, or how to get the most out of it. This blog fills that gap.

## What you will find here

- **Updates.** A short, plain-language look at each release: what is new, what got fixed and what it means for your materials.
- **Deep dives.** How parts of the Playground work under the hood, such as how a MaterialX document becomes a shader in your browser.
- **Tips.** Small workflows that save time in the viewer, the graph editor and the integrations.

Everything here is written for people who use MaterialX, whether you are new to it or ship it in production. If you are just starting out, the [What is MaterialX?]({{ appRoot }}#!what-is-materialx) page is a good first stop.

## How to follow along

Subscribe to the [Atom feed]({{ '/feed.xml' | url }}) in any feed reader, or check the latest posts on the [Playground home page]({{ appRoot }}#!home). Every post also has its own tags, so you can browse just the topics you care about from the [tags page]({{ '/tags/' | url }}).

## Feedback

Spotted a mistake, or want a topic covered? Open an issue on the [GitHub issue tracker](https://github.com/joaovbs96/MaterialXPlayground/issues). The Playground is free and open source, and suggestions shape what comes next on the [roadmap]({{ appRoot }}#!roadmap).
